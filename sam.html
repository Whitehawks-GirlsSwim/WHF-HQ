YOUR EVERYDAY CARDIO

Walk your way to healthier.

Start with 10–15 minutes of comfortable walking, 3 days a week. Build time gradually as it gets easier. Short walks count, too.

A long-term goal for most adults is 150 minutes of moderate activity weekly, plus 2 strength days.
🗓️ YOUR FIRST FOUR WEEKSSee easy plan ＋
Week 1

Walk or use a low-impact bike for 10 minutes, three times. Try one strength session and learn the movements.

Week 2

Move for 10–15 minutes, three or four times. Aim for two comfortable strength sessions.

Week 3

Try 15–20 minutes of movement, up to four days. Continue two strength days if recovery feels good.

Week 4

Keep the routine that fits your life. Add time gradually instead of making every workout harder.

Go slower when needed. The eventual general guideline is 150 minutes of moderate activity per week and two strength days, not a day-one requirement.

YOUR DAILY CALORIE TARGET

Enter your age, weight, height and activity level above to calculate your personal starting recommendation.

This is a general starting estimate, not a prescribed diet or a number you must stay under. It uses a standard adult-woman metabolism equation and estimated activity. If pregnant, breastfeeding, managing a medical condition, or using medications that affect appetite or weight, check a target with a healthcare professional. There’s no need to count calories to build healthier habits.

How this recommendation is calculated ＋

We estimate resting needs using the Mifflin–St Jeor equation, multiply by your chosen activity level for maintenance, then suggest a modest reduction of roughly 300–500 calories a day where appropriate. The estimate cannot account for all medical, medication, metabolic or lifestyle factors. Recalculate after meaningful weight or activity changes.

For individualized planning, try the NIH Body Weight Planner.

Getting support for a bigger goal ＋

A substantial weight-loss goal is worth discussing with a healthcare professional who can review health history, medications, blood pressure, sleep and nutrition and help confirm a healthy target.

Behavioral programs, registered dietitian support, and, when appropriate, prescription weight-management treatments or other options can be discussed together.

There is no fixed deadline. A common early goal is about 5–10% of starting weight over roughly six months, with the plan adjusted over time.

Based on general guidance from CDC Healthy Weight, CDC Activity Guidelines and NIDDK Body Weight Planner.
