More tips for swimmers ＋

Between swim + gym: Bring carbs and protein if sessions are close together.

Hydration: Swimmers still sweat. Drink regularly; sports drinks can be useful for long or hard practices. Avoid energy drinks.

Everyday nutrition: Include a variety of foods with iron, calcium, vitamin D and healthy fats.

Pay attention to recovery: Persistent fatigue, recurring injuries or missed periods warrant professional advice, not restriction.

Sources: Swimming Australia · Youth sports-nutrition review · AAP

BEFORE YOU LIFT

GYM PLAYBOOK

01 · Warm up

5–10 minutes of easy cycling or walking, followed by a few controlled practice reps for the first exercise.

02 · Find your starting weight

Suggested weights are just a place to start. Use one that leaves 3–4 clean reps in reserve. Machines don't all feel the same.

03 · Your sets

Two sets per exercise. Rest about 60–90 seconds between them. Keep every movement smooth and controlled.

04 · Get stronger

When every rep is easy and controlled, increase slightly next time. No need to max out, chase soreness or train to failure.

05 · Swim comes first

Keep gym days away from big races or especially tough swim practices when possible. Eat enough, hydrate and recover.

06 · Safety

Get a gym instructor to check machine settings and new free-weight movements. If there is pain, dizziness, or unusual severe muscle discomfort, stop training and tell a trusted adult or clinician.
