BEFORE PRACTICE

Bring energy to the pool.

Eat a regular meal a few hours beforehand. Closer to practice, choose a familiar carbohydrate snack, like toast, a banana, cereal, pretzels or applesauce.

🍌 Banana + toast🥣 Oatmeal🥨 Pretzels
BETWEEN SESSIONS

Back-to-back swim + gym?

Refuel with carbs and protein between sessions. A yogurt + granola, chocolate milk + a banana, or a sandwich work well when time is short.

🥛 Chocolate milk🥪 Sandwich🥣 Yogurt + granola
AFTER TRAINING

Recover. Rebuild. Repeat.

Have a snack or meal with carbohydrates and protein soon after practice, especially when dinner is delayed. Examples: rice bowl with chicken or tofu, eggs + toast + fruit, or a smoothie and sandwich.

🍚 Rice bowl🥚 Eggs + toast🥤 Smoothie
MEET-DAY KIT

Pack your fuel.

Bring familiar, easy-to-digest foods to long meets. Eat between races when there is time, keep a water bottle handy, and have a bigger recovery meal after your last event.

🍎 Fruit🥯 Bagel🥨 Crackers💧 Water bottle
DID YOU KNOW?

Pool water hides sweat.

Swimmers still need fluid. Drink regularly throughout the day and around practice. On long or intense days, a sports drink can be useful, but water is usually enough for lighter sessions. Energy drinks are not recommended for teens.

Good nutrition is more than macros.

Include varied foods with iron, calcium, vitamin D and healthy fats. Fatigue, recurrent injury, missed periods or persistent low energy are reasons to talk with a health professional or sports dietitian, not to cut food.

Sources: Swimming Australia · Youth athlete nutrition review · American Academy of Pediatrics
BEFORE YOU LIFT

GYM PLAYBOOK

01 · Warm up

5–10 minutes of easy cycling or walking, followed by a few controlled practice reps for the first exercise.

02 · Find your starting weight

Suggested weights are just a place to start. Use one that leaves 3–4 clean reps in reserve. Machines don't all feel the same.

03 · Your sets

Two sets per exercise. Rest about 60–90 seconds between them. Keep every movement smooth and controlled.

04 · Get stronger

When every rep is easy and controlled, increase slightly next time. No need to max out, chase soreness or train to failure.

05 · Swim comes first

Keep gym days away from big races or especially tough swim practices when possible. Eat enough, hydrate and recover.

06 · Safety

Get a gym instructor to check machine settings and new free-weight movements. If there is pain, dizziness, or unusual severe muscle discomfort, stop training and tell a trusted adult or clinician.
